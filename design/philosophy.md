# Kinetic Cartography

A movement about motion made legible. It treats velocity not as blur but as structure: the patient, measured record of things travelling from one point to another, drawn with the exactness of a surveyor and the restraint of a typesetter.

**Space and form.** Compositions are built on an invisible grid of streets — long parallel strokes, a few decisive diagonals, and nodes where paths meet. Lines are thin, constant in weight, and never decorative; each one reads as a route that was actually taken. Form emerges from accumulation: hundreds of identical marks laid down one after another until density itself becomes the image. Every stroke must look meticulously placed, the product of painstaking attention rather than a generated pattern.

**Colour and material.** A deep night-ink ground — the colour of a city seen from above after sunset — carries a single signal colour: a burning, saturated orange that marks movement, arrival, and urgency. Everything else is quiet: bone-white at low opacity, cool slate for structure. Orange is used sparingly, so that wherever it appears the eye knows something is happening there. The palette is calibrated with master-level care; no tint exists without a reason.

**Scale and rhythm.** Rhythm comes from repetition at intervals: dashes on a lane, dots marking stops, ticks along a measured axis. Large calm fields sit against small dense clusters, so the work breathes and then accelerates. The cadence should feel like a well-run dispatch floor — steady, timed, relentless, never chaotic.

**Composition and balance.** Weight flows in one direction, as if the whole field is being pulled toward a destination just beyond the frame. Asymmetry is deliberate; the heaviest density sits off-centre, and generous negative space gives the motion somewhere to go. Nothing touches an edge it should not touch; margins are honoured with the discipline of a craftsman who has refined the layout countless times.

**Hierarchy and text.** Words are rare and clinical — a coordinate, an index number, a single word set small and spaced wide, like annotations on a survey plate. Type never explains; it anchors. The information lives in the lines, the dots, the single orange path. The finished piece should feel like an artifact from an imaginary discipline devoted to measuring speed — the work of someone at the very top of their field, executed with uncompromising precision.
